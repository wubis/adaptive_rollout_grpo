# Project review and research directions · November 7, 2026

**Recommendation:** retire prompt-specific continuation length as the main contribution on the present model/population. Keep the replay infrastructure and use it to investigate whether adaptive allocation preferentially selects unreliable reward contrasts. The strongest affordable next step is an audit and a small controlled comparison of allocation rules that account for verification uncertainty. A second, more expensive direction is deciding when to extend an unfinished answer versus sample another answer. These are proposals; neither has demonstrated an improvement here.

This review covers the working tree, the completed calibration cache `artifacts/replay.VgSpgmeu/`, the prior training pilot, and primary research available by November 7. Literature search included recent October/November work, rather than only the papers already named in the project notes. Original protocols and results have not been changed, and no GPU collection was launched. The companion [diagnostic script](review_calibration_diagnostics.py) reproduces the saved development report exactly and writes the additional [post-hoc diagnostics](2026-11-07-diagnostics.json).

**What the project has established.** The engineering foundation is useful: a functioning fixed-8 update, pinned model and decoding, raw completion/token retention, recovery accounting, a history-limited replay dispatcher, disjoint prompt manifests, and predeclared advancement criteria. The most recent run is a frozen-policy calibration study, not an adaptive training run.

The earlier online pilot made four updates using 22,660 generated training tokens. Validation was 8/8 before and after. That validates update mechanics but establishes neither a learning gain nor an adequate validation set. The variable-size online path remains work: `fixed8.group_advantages` requires exactly eight rewards, while `smoke_fixed8.update_policy` divides by `len(groups) * GROUP_SIZE`. Connecting replay groups of size 4/8/12/16 directly would violate the intended equal-prompt weighting.

**Additional findings from the cache.** These were computed for this review and are post-hoc, with original recorded rewards unchanged.

1. **Better cost prediction alone has little room at the current decision boundary.** Replace a continuation's estimated cost with its actual next-chunk cost, while keeping the fitted mix probabilities and pooled fresh cost fixed. Only **1 of 35 observed continuation states** changes its ranking against a fresh action. For all-fail states after four samples, actual costs are 930–1,536 tokens but the break-even cost is only 538. This is not a global oracle bound: it does not optimize whole trajectories, compare all continuation substitutions, or allow new probability models. It does show why another length regressor is a weak first investment.
2. **The two uniform outcomes behave very differently.** Of nine initially all-fail prompts, four eventually mix. All eleven initially all-correct prompts eventually mix. In nine of those eleven, the first opposing reward is an **unparseable** completion; only two are parseable incorrect answers. There are four truncated and five nontruncated unparseable cases. The pattern warrants adjudication; it does not prove nine verifier errors.
3. **The actual simulated allocations exploit the all-correct side.** Across the five token-cost trajectories, continuation chunk counts are 1, 4, 0, 4, and 4, all on initially all-correct prompts. No initially all-fail prompt receives a continuation. Of the retained mixed groups, respectively 9/21, 7/21, 6/20, 10/23, and 6/18 have no parseable negative answer. The same prompts recur across orders. This is a concrete reason to separate reliable mathematical contrast from raw verifier disagreement.

Two further boundaries matter. [DAPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476) already studies dynamic filtering and overlong reward handling, and [Dr. GRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20783) examines normalization biases. [TRACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11119v2) allocates at prompt roots and agentic prefixes, so moving to trees is an expensive pivot with substantial prior art. [Temporal Scheduling for RLVR](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25381v1) also makes changing token-credit priorities over training an established direction. These are useful controls or context, not reasons to implement every method.

**Direction 1 — allocation that accounts for verification uncertainty. Recommended.**

Research question: *Does a scheduler maximizing observed mixed groups disproportionately allocate to answer-extraction failures, and can selective rechecking improve adjudicated contrast per total compute?*

Keep the original binary task reward and attach separate metadata: clear answer, parser failure, ambiguous answer selection, and truncation before a complete answer. An unparseable output remains a failure under the existing benchmark contract; it is not automatically relabeled correct. The research question is whether this type of negative provides the same useful signal as a confidently wrong mathematical conclusion.

Start with two actions already implemented: fresh sampling and continuation. Add a bounded recheck action only for ambiguous completions that can change a group's mixed/uniform status or the next dispatch. Rechecking should select an answer without consulting gold, then apply equivalence checking. A human adjudicator can establish evaluation labels; an online policy needs a frozen automated recheck rule. Disagreement between two parsers is a flag, not calibrated confidence.

A simple candidate scores expected **reliably adjudicated** contrast per generation-plus-recheck cost, estimated from development data. Compare ordinary pooled-cost scheduling, a simple “defer ambiguous groups” rule, and selective rechecking at the same recheck budget. Include random rechecking to test whether targeting matters. Keep this small; fitting a large uncertainty model to twenty uniform prompts is not credible.

Evaluate both changes of group status and changes of allocation, audited mixed groups per generated token, recheck time/model tokens, initially all-fail coverage, and sensitivity of the method ranking to unresolved judgments. Genuine output-format improvement can be useful; report it separately from mathematical accuracy. A win in collection quality still needs a training comparison to establish learning benefit.

The proposed distinction from existing noise correction is **adaptive selection amplifying or avoiding structured verification errors**, measured under a fixed budget. It remains a novelty hypothesis. A useful negative result is also possible: targeted rechecks might barely change allocation, in which case the project becomes a well-controlled measurement study rather than a new scheduler.

For intuition only, if every response is truly correct and an independent verifier flips a reward with probability e, the observed probability of a mixed group of size g is `1 - (1-e)^g - e^g`. At an illustrative e=0.02, this is about 7.8% for g=4 and 27.6% for g=16. These are mathematical examples, not estimates of this verifier's error rate. Real errors can be correlated and systematic.

**Direction 2 — decide between more answers and more time per answer. Strong mechanism, greater scope.**

Research question: *For groups dominated by unfinished outputs, does extending an existing completion recover useful mathematical answers more efficiently than drawing another short completion?* The 39.3% truncation rate makes this mechanism relevant, but does not show that longer generation will help.

First collect a small paired cap study with a frozen checkpoint and longer maximum outputs, retaining prefixes at 384 tokens. One illustrative development design is 24 new prompts × 8 completions × a 1,024-token cap: at most 196,608 generated tokens. Analyze who acquires a final answer after the short cap, whether it is correct, and the additional tokens required. Cap increases must be compared at equal total tokens, and a censored answer must not be assumed ultimately correct.

If the measurement supports it, add a choice among a fresh prompt, another independent answer, or continuation of an unfinished answer. Compare fixed short/long caps and a fixed extension rule before a learned selector. Preserve the generating checkpoint for resumed suffixes and record segment provenance. The existing 384-token cache contains no suffix evidence, so this cannot be validated retrospectively from it. DUET and DAPO are essential comparators; the proposed distinction is selective extension of unfinished work, not generic length control. Avoid combining this with Direction 1 in the first experiment.

**Direction 3 — test whether mixed-group yield predicts useful updates. Best connection to the course anchor.**

Research question: *At equal generation cost, do extra mixed groups improve the gradient estimate or generalization, especially when they arise from all-correct prompts?*

Use the saved token sequences and pinned checkpoint for a small gradient study. Compare groups with confidently correct and confidently incorrect answers, groups whose negative side is unparseable, and groups obtained after an initially all-fail probe. Match group size, sample counts, loss weighting, and length strata. Measure gradient variance and alignment to a disjoint, carefully scored reference batch. Any restricted-layer gradient is only a proxy and should be labeled as such. Neither a larger gradient norm nor positive cosine similarity proves improved accuracy.

A possible intervention reserves a small fraction of tokens for initially all-fail groups rather than spending every optional chunk on immediately attractive contrast. Sweep only a few prespecified fractions, report the coverage/yield tradeoff, and compare to a simple random exploration quota. CoBA-RL and AdaRoll already motivate capability/utility allocation, so the contribution would be a measured failure of the current proxy and a minimal correction. This route needs GPU gradient work and eventually online learning; it is less affordable than an audit.

There is an immediate implementation warning: with binary rewards and population-standard-deviation normalization, a nonuniform group's sum of squared advantages is approximately its size. Calling this quantity “learning value” mostly repackages group size unless additional information is introduced.

**Direction 4 — a reproducible negative result and decision-boundary study. Lowest cost.**

Research question: *When can per-prompt cost estimation actually change a sequential rollout allocation?* Extend the existing break-even analysis across development populations, caps, and checkpoints, then replicate one frozen comparison on fresh prompts. Report prediction error alongside boundary crossings, coverage, and yield. The useful contribution is a diagnostic showing when estimator improvements are operationally irrelevant. This supports a course project with a clear empirical story, but does not claim a new frontier algorithm. Whether a replay-centered project satisfies the course's improvement requirement remains a TA decision documented as outstanding in PROJECT_CONTEXT.md.

**Recommended next experiment and stopping rules.**

| Stage | Concrete work | Decision it enables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing cache, no new generation | Adjudicate the eleven first opposing outcomes on initially all-correct prompts, both known failures, and complete groups sampled from each contrast type. Add a random audit component if estimating prevalence; keep targeted cases separate. | Determine whether the observed pattern is mostly genuine wrong answers, incomplete answers, or extraction mistakes. |
| Existing cache, CPU replay | Compare original and adjudicated group states; test pooled scheduling, simple ambiguity deferral, and a bounded targeted-recheck rule. Labels used for evaluation must not leak into dispatch. | Establish that a proposed mechanism can change decisions and improve the intended metric. |
| Fresh development only if needed | Obtain a separate modest sample to freeze the rule and verify answer selection. If the cap is the main failure, perform the paired cap study instead. | Avoid endlessly tuning thresholds on the same sixty prompts. |
| One new held-out study | Freeze one candidate, one strong simple control, accounting, audit protocol, and a minimum useful margin before collection. | Test the chosen claim independently. |
| Online only after an informative baseline | Replace the ceiling validation set; validate variable-size loss weighting; show the affordable fixed-8 baseline can move a development metric. Then run a budget-matched pair. | Support a learning-efficiency claim rather than only collection efficiency. |

Keep the original twenty held-out level-1–3 prompts reserved. They do not by themselves represent the newer balanced level-1–5 population, so any use must state that target-population difference. Reordering a cache is a sensitivity check; bootstrap whole prompts if quantifying prompt uncertainty, and use independently sampled prompts/rollouts for stronger replication. Reserve final benchmark data for the final frozen protocol.

For a new rule, replace the length-MAE gate with checks appropriate to its mechanism, declared before new evaluation. Useful provisional targets are a nontrivial number of distinct changed decisions, at least a 10% improvement in independently adjudicated contrast per cost over the strongest simple rule, and no reversal under plausible treatment of unresolved labels. Set minimum counts and uncertainty handling from the new design's attainable sample size; these targets are not statistically powered guarantees. Do not retroactively reinterpret the previous failed gates as passed.

The recorded pilot projects roughly 2.52 A100 hours per million generated-and-optimized tokens. A pair of 0.5-million-token training runs therefore projects about 2.52 GPU-hours for generation and optimization alone; three paired seeds project 7.56 hours, before evaluation, setup, calibration, and recovery. This is a hardware/path-specific estimate, not evidence that this training budget will produce measurable learning. For the 196,608-token cap study, applying the observed calibration throughput gives about 25 minutes of generation, but longer sequences and different batch shapes can change throughput.

The minimum useful project package is the original null result, the new decision-boundary and reward-source diagnostics, one clearly motivated intervention, and a fresh evaluation with appropriately limited claims. The proposed work should be chosen and developed by the team, and AI assistance disclosed as already required by the course notes.
